# Textual Composition of the Book of Mormon (Geography-Relevant Framework)

*Draft for the research atlas and eventual book. Updated 2026-07-29.*

## Why composition matters for geography

Geographic statements do not all come from the same editorial layer. Understanding *who wrote* a passage and *how it was preserved* affects how strongly we treat first-person travel reports versus later abridgments summarizing centuries.

## Major layers

### 1. Small Plates of Nephi (largely intact)

**Contents in the current book:** 1 Nephi, 2 Nephi, Jacob, Enos, Jarom, Omni, plus Mormon’s brief bridge in Words of Mormon.

**Status:** After the loss of the 116 pages (Mormon’s abridgment of the early large-plate record covering roughly Lehi through early Benjamin), Mormon inserted the small plates he had discovered. These plates were kept by Nephi and successors as a religious/ministry record and were **not** re-abridged by Mormon into a new narrative voice for those books.

**Implication for geography:** Early journey accounts (Jerusalem → wilderness → Bountiful → ocean voyage → landing and first planting) are first-person or near-contemporary. Directional language, “many days,” shipbuilding environment, and seed-planting success are high-signal internal data.

### 2. Mormon’s abridgment of the large plates

**Contents:** Mosiah through Mormon (bulk of the middle history), drawn from the large plates of Nephi and related sources.

**Implication:** Place-names, wars, and relative geography are often summarized. Distances may be schematic. Still rich for relative location graphs (Zarahemla–Nephi–Bountiful–Desolation, Sidon, narrow neck).

### 3. Moroni

**Contents:** Completion of Mormon’s record; Moroni’s own books; abridgment of Ether (Jaredite).

**Implication:** Jaredite geography may use different naming and scale. Final Cumorah/Ramah statements sit at the end of both Nephite and Jaredite arcs—critical for model discrimination.

### 4. Title Page / structural notes

Moroni’s title page and editorial asides sometimes frame purpose more than maps, but still constrain how “this land” language is read.

## Working principles for this project

1. **Catalog before conclude.** Every geographic verse gets a record; models attach *claims* to records; readers weigh them.
2. **Separate tracks.** Model claims vs independent insights (climate, travel times, hydrology, history) live in parallel.
3. **Plate-source tagging.** Tag `small-plates` vs `mormon-abridgment` vs `moroni`/`ether`.
4. **No single-model advocacy in core data.** Book prose may later argue; the data layer stays comparative.

## High-level narrative geography skeleton (internal)

| Phase | Rough books | Geographic themes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old World journey | 1 Nephi 1–18 | Wilderness, Bountiful (OW), ship, voyage |
| Landing & first settlement | 1 Nephi 18–22; 2 Nephi | Seeds, promised land, splits |
| Early Nephite/Lamanite | Jacob–Omni; Mosiah | Land of Nephi, Zeniff, Zarahemla contact |
| Classic Nephite | Alma–Helaman | Sidon, wars, Bountiful, narrow neck, northward |
| Decline & end | 3 Nephi–Mormon | Gathering, final wars, Cumorah |
| Jaredite flashback | Ether | Older civilization, Ramah/Cumorah overlap |
