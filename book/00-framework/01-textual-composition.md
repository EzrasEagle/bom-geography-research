# Textual Composition of the Book of Mormon (Geography-Relevant Framework)

*Draft for the research atlas and eventual book. Revised 2026-07-29 (chronology correction).*

## Why composition matters for geography

Geographic statements do not all come from the same editorial layer. Understanding *who wrote* a passage and *how it was preserved* affects how strongly we treat first-person travel reports versus later abridgments summarizing centuries.

## Critical chronology (do not conflate)

Two separate events, ~1,400 years apart, are often mixed up:

| When | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~A.D. 385–400 | **Mormon** (and then Moroni) | Mormon abridges the **large plates** of Nephi into his own plates. He also finds and **includes the small plates of Nephi** with his record, with **Words of Mormon** as his bridge and explanation (W of M 1:3–7). He says he does this for a **wise purpose** according to the Spirit—not because of any modern manuscript loss. |
| A.D. 1828 | **Joseph Smith / Martin Harris** | Joseph translates Mormon’s abridgment of the early large-plate material (often called the **Book of Lehi**). About **116 pages** of that manuscript are **lost**. The Lord commands that the portion not be retranslated the same way (D&C 10). The **already-included small plates** (which Mormon had placed on the plates ~1,400 years earlier) cover the same historical span from a different, more spiritual/ministry angle and become 1 Nephi–Omni in the printed book. |

**Correct summary:** Mormon did *not* insert the small plates “after” the 116-page loss. The 116-page loss is a **nineteenth-century translation/manuscript event**. Mormon’s inclusion of the small plates is a **fourth-century editorial decision** on the plates themselves, later providential relative to the modern loss.

## Major layers

### 1. Small Plates of Nephi (largely intact in the printed book)

**Contents:** 1 Nephi, 2 Nephi, Jacob, Enos, Jarom, Omni, plus Mormon’s short **Words of Mormon**.

**Ancient editorial status:** Mormon discovered these plates while abridging and chose to put them with his record (W of M 1:3–6). They were kept by Nephi and successors primarily as a religious/ministry record. They were **not** re-written by Mormon into a new abridged voice for those books.

**Modern recovery status:** After the 116 pages (Book of Lehi abridgment) were lost in 1828, the small-plate account supplies the early narrative in the published Book of Mormon instead of a retranslation of the lost abridgment.

**Implication for geography:** Early journey accounts (Jerusalem → wilderness → Old World Bountiful → ocean voyage → landing and first planting) remain first-person or near-contemporary on the small plates. Directional language, “many days,” shipbuilding environment, and seed-planting success are high-signal internal data—regardless of the later 1828 loss.

### 2. Mormon’s abridgment of the large plates

**Contents:** Mosiah through Mormon (bulk of the middle history), drawn from the large plates of Nephi and related sources in ~4th century A.D.

**Implication:** Place-names, wars, and relative geography are often summarized. Distances may be schematic. Still rich for relative place graphs (Zarahemla–Nephi–Bountiful–Desolation, Sidon, narrow neck).

### 3. Moroni

**Contents:** Completion of Mormon’s record; Moroni’s own books; abridgment of Ether (Jaredite).

**Implication:** Jaredite geography may use different naming and scale. Final Cumorah/Ramah statements sit at the end of both Nephite and Jaredite arcs—critical for model discrimination.

### 4. Title Page / structural notes

Moroni’s title page and editorial asides sometimes frame purpose more than maps, but still constrain how “this land” language is read.

## Working principles for this project

1. **Catalog before conclude.** Every geographic verse gets a record; models attach *claims* (with **assumptions**) to records; readers weigh them.
2. **Separate tracks.** Model claims vs independent insights (climate, travel, hydrology, language, genetics, artifacts, history).
3. **Plate-source tagging.** Tag `small-plates` vs `mormon-abridgment` vs `moroni`/`ether`.
4. **No single-model advocacy in core data.** Book prose may argue; the data layer stays comparative.
5. **Assumptions are first-class.** Every mapping (e.g. “Sidon = Mississippi”) is an explicit, editable assumption, not hidden glue.
6. **User models are first-class.** Users may fork published models, change assumptions, tag verses, and optionally share.

## High-level narrative geography skeleton (internal)

| Phase | Rough books | Geographic themes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old World journey | 1 Nephi 1–18 | Wilderness, Bountiful (OW), ship, voyage |
| Landing & first settlement | 1 Nephi 18–22; 2 Nephi | Seeds, promised land, splits |
| Early Nephite/Lamanite | Jacob–Omni; Mosiah | Land of Nephi, Zeniff, Zarahemla contact |
| Classic Nephite | Alma–Helaman | Sidon, wars, Bountiful, narrow neck, northward |
| Decline & end | 3 Nephi–Mormon | Gathering, final wars, Cumorah |
| Jaredite flashback | Ether | Older civilization, Ramah/Cumorah overlap |
