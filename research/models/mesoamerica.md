# Limited Mesoamerica Model

## Summary
Places Book of Mormon events primarily in southern Mexico and Guatemala (Mesoamerica), with a limited geography rather than hemispheric scale. The Isthmus of Tehuantepec is commonly proposed as the narrow neck of land.

## Core map (typical mappings — vary by author)
| Feature | Common proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Land southward | Highland Guatemala / southern Mexico |
| Land northward | Central / Gulf lowlands north of isthmus |
| Narrow neck | Isthmus of Tehuantepec |
| River Sidon | Grijalva (or Usumacinta in alternate variants) |
| Landing (Lehi) | Pacific coast of Mesoamerica (debated) |
| Cumorah (final) | Southern Mexico hill country (not NY for final battle in many versions) |

## Key claims
- Distances in the text fit a limited (~few hundred miles) stage.
- Culture level, cities, warfare, and writing systems align better with Mesoamerican civilizations than with temperate North America alone.
- Directional conventions and “up/down” can map to highland/lowland topography.

## Strengths claimed by proponents
- Archaeological complexity and time depth.
- Narrow-neck geography candidates.
- Cultural parallels (cement, large populations, etc.).

## Criticisms commonly raised
- Hill Cumorah tradition in New York vs. southern final battle.
- Directional quirks (some maps rotate “north”).
- Specific river/city correlations remain contested among Mesoamericanists themselves.

## Primary sources (seed)
