# Heartland Model

## Summary
Locates primary Book of Mormon lands in the central and eastern United States (and adjacent areas), often with the Mississippi as Sidon and Great Lakes / river systems framing seas or boundaries. Emphasizes prophetic statements about the United States and Hopewell/Adena archaeology.

## Core map (typical)
| Feature | Common proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Land southward | Southeastern US / lower Mississippi |
| Land northward | Great Lakes / upper Midwest |
| Narrow neck | Niagara / Great Lakes corridor (variants differ) |
| River Sidon | Mississippi River |
| Landing | Gulf Coast / Florida / Atlantic (variants) |
| Cumorah | New York (same hill as modern tradition) |

## Key claims
- New York Cumorah is both repository and final battle region.
- Prophetic statements about this land of promise apply to the United States.
- Hopewell cultural horizon used as external correlation window.

## Strengths claimed
- Alignment with some modern prophetic/tradition sources on Cumorah location.
- Major river systems matching travel narratives at continental scale.

## Criticisms commonly raised
- Population and urban complexity vs. text.
- “Sea west / sea east” and narrow neck fit debated.
- Distances and climate for some crops/war seasons contested.

## Primary sources (seed)
- Meldrum, Rodney (various Heartland conferences / books).
- May, Wayne (various).
- Critiques in scholarly LDS journals (for balance).
