# On-foot route — Baja California to the Gulf of Mexico

## Why this insight exists
Independent geographic literacy for anyone testing west-coast landing models, continental travel claims, or hydrology. Not a model endorsement.

## Land connection constraint
Baja is a peninsula. Continuous foot travel to the Gulf of Mexico requires the northern land bridge via the Colorado River Delta / Laguna Salada lowlands into mainland Sonora—not a crossing of the Gulf of California.

## Preferred natural corridor (summary)
1. Northern Baja / eastern Peninsular Ranges → Colorado Delta lowlands  
2. Sonoran Desert eastward (tinajas, arroyos, sky islands; optional Gila corridor in borderlands)  
3. Basin-and-range valleys of the Chihuahuan transition  
4. Rio Grande / Rio Bravo corridor downstream to the Gulf mouth  

Mexican-only variant: northern Sierra Madre Occidental passes → Rio Conchos → Rio Grande → Gulf.

## Distance & modern experienced travelers
Straight-line northern Baja area to Rio Grande mouth ~1,175 miles; realistic path often **1,500–2,500+ miles**. Multi-month expedition; cool season essential for desert legs.

## 18th-century peoples (ideal timing)
With horses/mules (standard on northern New Spain frontier), small experienced party, cool season, local water knowledge: about **4–5 months**. Pure foot typically **5–7 months**. Anchors: Anza-era daily averages (~10–15 mi/day overall with rests), Domínguez–Escalante scale marches.
